God Is Not a Man – Why Jesus Is Not God (From the Bible)

January 14, 2025 • 10 min read • By Lorem Ipsum

This article argues directly from the Bible that God is one, that He is not a man, cannot become a weak human, cannot die, and cannot be powerless – and therefore that Jesus, who was a man that ate, slept, prayed and died, is not God but a servant of God.

1. One God Only – No Partners, No “Persons” Beside Him

The foundation of the Bible is uncompromising monotheism. God is one, unique, and alone in His Godhood. No “second person” or “third person” shares His status.

Deuteronomy 6:4 – “Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD.”

Isaiah 44:6 – “I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God.”

Isaiah 45:5 – “I am the LORD, and there is none else, there is no God beside me.”

1 Corinthians 8:4 – “There is none other God but one.”

1 Timothy 2:5 – “For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.”

These verses crush the idea of multiple “co-equal divine persons.” God says there is no other God beside Him. Jesus is clearly described as a man and as a mediator between men and the one God – not as God Himself.

· · ·
2. God Is Not a Man – Jesus Was

The Bible draws a sharp line: God is not a human being. Jesus, on the other hand, is called a man and “son of man” again and again.

Numbers 23:19 – “God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent.”

Hosea 11:9 – “For I am God, and not man; the Holy One in the midst of thee.”

1 Samuel 15:29 – “The Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent: for he is not a man, that he should repent.”

These are not vague poetic lines. They explicitly deny that God is “a man” or “son of man.” Jesus is constantly called “son of man” (for example, Matthew 12:8, Mark 2:10, Luke 9:22). The Bible says God is not that.

To say “God became a man” directly flips these verses. The Scripture says God is not a man; later theology says God did become a man. You cannot make both true at the same time.

· · ·
3. God Is Spirit – Not Flesh and Blood

God’s nature is spirit, not flesh. Spirit is not a Jewish male body walking in Galilee. Jesus had flesh and blood, ate food, and grew tired.

John 4:24 – “God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.”

Luke 24:39 – Jesus says: “Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.”

Jesus himself makes the distinction: a spirit does not have flesh and bones. God is a spirit. Jesus openly shows that he has flesh and bones. Therefore Jesus is not God. To claim “God became flesh” is to tear apart Jesus’ own words.

· · ·
4. God Alone Is Immortal – He Does Not Die

The Bible describes God as eternal and immortal. Death belongs to created, limited beings – not to the One who gives life to all.

1 Timothy 6:16 – God “only hath immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto.”

1 Timothy 1:17 – “Now unto the King eternal, immortal, invisible, the only wise God, be honour and glory for ever and ever. Amen.”

Habakkuk 1:12 – “Art thou not from everlasting, O LORD my God, mine Holy One? we shall not die.”

Psalm 102:27 – “But thou art the same, and thy years shall have no end.”

Immortal means not subject to death. Yet Jesus was killed and died on the cross, then was buried. If you say “Jesus is God,” you are forced to say “God died” – which clashes head-on with “God only hath immortality” and “thy years shall have no end.”

Christians try to escape this by saying “only his human nature died.” But that means it was a man dying, not the immortal God. You cannot honestly claim “God died for your sins” while also insisting that God cannot die.

· · ·
5. God Is All-Powerful – Never Weak or Powerless

Scripture presents God as absolutely powerful. No one can overpower Him, defeat Him, or force Him.

Job 42:2 – “I know that thou canst do every thing, and that no thought can be withholden from thee.”

Jeremiah 32:17 – “Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee.”

Luke 1:37 – “For with God nothing shall be impossible.”

Matthew 19:26 – “With men this is impossible; but with God all things are possible.”

Yet Jesus is clearly limited and weak: he becomes hungry, thirsty, tired; he is arrested, beaten, mocked, and finally killed by his enemies. He cries out and prays for rescue.

A being who can be overpowered, beaten, and killed is not the Almighty God of Job 42:2 and Jeremiah 32:17. It is a dependent servant of God, relying on the true Almighty above him.

· · ·
6. “Son of God” Is a Title – Not a Literal Divine Offspring

The Bible itself uses “son of God” in a broad, non-divine way. It is a title of honour, covenant and closeness, not a biological or metaphysical “second God.”

Exodus 4:22 – “Israel is my son, even my firstborn.”

Hosea 11:1 – “When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt.”

2 Samuel 7:14 – God about the king: “I will be his father, and he shall be my son.”

Psalm 2:7 – “Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee.”

Romans 8:14 – “For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God.”

If “son of God” automatically meant “this person is God,” then Israel would be God, David would be God, and every believer led by the Spirit would be God. Clearly that is not the meaning.

The phrase “Son of God” for Jesus means he is the chosen Messiah and honoured servant of God – not that he is the one God of Deuteronomy 6:4.

· · ·
7. Jesus Himself Separates Between Himself and God

If anyone had the right to say “I am God, worship me,” it would be Jesus. Instead, he constantly points to “God” as someone above him.

John 20:17 – Jesus says: “I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God.”

John 14:28 – “My Father is greater than I.”

Mark 13:32 – About the Last Day: “Neither the Son, but the Father.”

Luke 6:12 – “He went out into a mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God.”

Acts 2:22 – “Jesus of Nazareth, a man approved of God among you by miracles and wonders and signs, which God did by him.”

Acts 2:36 – “God hath made that same Jesus… both Lord and Christ.”

1 Corinthians 8:6 – “But to us there is but one God, the Father… and one Lord Jesus Christ.”

Jesus has a God above him. He prays to that God. He says that God is greater than him. He is described openly as a man approved of God and as someone whom God made Lord and Christ. This is not how the Bible speaks about the one Almighty God – this is how it speaks about a prophet, servant, and Messiah.

Therefore the honest conclusion from these verses is simple and sharp: Jesus is not God, and God is not Jesus. The one true God is the One whom Jesus worshipped, obeyed, and called “my God and your God.”
